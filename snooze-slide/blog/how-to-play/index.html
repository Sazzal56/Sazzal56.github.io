How to play Snooze Slide: four rules and you know the whole game

1 October 2026 · by the developer, Lamplit Games

I make Snooze Slide on my own, and the rule I set myself was that nobody should need to read anything to play it. So this page is for the curious: the whole game is four rules, and you will have met all four by the end of level 5.

Rule 1: one swipe moves every cat

Swipe anywhere on the screen: up, down, left or right. Every cat that is awake slides that way, all at once, and keeps sliding until something stops it. A wall stops a cat. So does a plant pot, and so does another cat.

You never pick a cat. That is the whole puzzle: you can only move them together, so you use the room to pull them apart.

Rule 2: a cat has to stop on its own bed

Each cat has a bed in its own colour. When a cat stops on its bed, it curls up and falls asleep. Sliding across the bed does not count. If nothing is there to stop the cat, it slides right over its bed and carries on to the wall.

So the real question on most levels is not "how do I get there?" but "what will stop me there?"

Rule 3: a sleeping cat is a wall

A cat that is asleep stays where it is for the rest of the level. Other cats bump into it and stop. Very often that is the answer to rule 2: put one cat to bed first, and it becomes the stopper the next cat needs.

Rule 4: so the order matters

Because sleeping cats are walls, the order you put them to bed in changes the room. The same wall that helps one cat can block another from its bed for good. If that happens the game tells you straight away, and you can undo as many swipes as you like. There is no timer and there are no lives.

What you meet as you go from room to room

The house has ten rooms with twenty levels each. The first five rooms each bring one new thing:

  • Bedroom: the four rules above, and nothing else.
  • Kitchen: cushions. A cat that slides onto a cushion stops on it.
  • Living room: cardboard boxes, in pairs. A cat that slides into one box comes out of the other and keeps sliding.
  • Library: cat flaps. They open one way only, the way the arrow points. From any other side a flap is a wall.
  • Attic: balls of yarn. Bump one and it rolls away until something stops it, while the cat that bumped it stays where it is. A ball that has stopped is one more thing to stop a cat.

The five rooms upstairs mix these together.

Stars, and why "best" really is the best

Every level shows a number called Best: the fewest swipes it can be solved in. Match it and you get three stars. Up to two swipes more is two stars, and any win is one star. Each level also has a swipe limit a few above Best; if you run out, undo a swipe or start the level again.

That number is not a guess. Before a level goes into the game, a program plays out every possible position and finds the shortest solution, so when the game says it can be done in five swipes, it can, and it cannot be done in four.

The Daily Bedtime puzzle

After level 5 the Daily opens: one new puzzle each day, the same one for everyone. It is a good one to send to a friend.

If you get stuck anywhere, the lightbulb button shows the next swipe. You start with three hints, solving a Daily earns one more, and after that a hint costs one short ad that you choose to watch.
